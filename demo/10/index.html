A research memo

Hold the trial. Run the model.

A fermata is the instruction to hold a note longer than its written value — a deliberate pause, decided in advance, because the passage needs it. We think the industry needs one before the point where a programme becomes expensive to be wrong about.

The thesis

The cheapest place to be wrong is inside a model.

Ninety percent of clinical failures are decisions made years earlier with information that was available and not modelled. We are not claiming the model is right. We are claiming that being wrong in simulation costs four orders of magnitude less than being wrong in Phase II, and that the gap is worth building for.

Four commitments
01

Multimodal

Several kinds of measurement, one account of what the tissue is doing.

02

Multi-scale

Molecule, cell, tissue — the same model, carried through, without the usual handoffs.

03

Causal

Trained on interventions, so that it can be asked about one.

04

Mechanistic

With the pathway attached, because a number without a mechanism cannot be argued with.

What we are building
Subject

The virtual tissue

Cells in context rather than cells in isolation. The context is the part the clinic tests and the dish omits.

Application

Target discovery

Multi-omics target discovery where candidates are scored by simulated tissue response before anyone commits a programme to them.

Position

Computational tech-bio

A small group in Vancouver, building the model layer. Deliberately unhurried about everything except rigour.

A quiet lab, doing loud arithmetic.
