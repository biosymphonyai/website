Simulating disease*

A virtual tissue, built to be wrong in measurable ways.

Most models of biology are unfalsifiable in practice: broad enough to explain any outcome, evaluated on data they were fit to. We are building the other kind — multimodal, multi-scale, causal, mechanistic, and specific enough to be caught out.

The thesis

If it cannot fail a prediction, it is not a model. It is a description.

The measure we care about is held-out perturbation accuracy in unseen tissue. It is a harder benchmark than the field usually reports, it makes early results look worse, and it is the only number that has any relationship to whether a target survives Phase II.

Four commitments
01

Multimodal

Transcriptomic, proteomic, epigenomic and spatial channels in one representation. Modality dropout is a stated failure mode, not a footnote.

02

Multi-scale

Molecular → cellular → tissue, in one model. Scale handoffs are where information is silently discarded.

03

Causal

Perturbation-trained. Counterfactual queries are the intended interface; correlation is a baseline we report against.

04

Mechanistic

Predictions carry pathway-level attribution. Unattributable predictions are logged as such.

What we are building
Object

The virtual tissue

Not a virtual cell. Cells in context — neighbours, gradients, matrix, feedback — because context is what the clinic tests and the dish does not.

Output

Targets, with error bars

Target discovery across multi-omics, delivered with the model's uncertainty and the perturbations it failed to predict.

Method

Evaluation before scale

We would rather have a small model with an honest benchmark than a large one with a leaderboard.

* Simulating disease, in the narrow sense: predicting how a perturbation propagates through modelled tissue, and being specific enough about it that the prediction can be shown false.
