Orchestrating the signals of life

Every cell in concert.

An orchestra is not a set of soloists in a room. What it produces cannot be recovered from the parts, only from the arrangement — who enters, when, against what. Tissue is the same object, and we are modelling it that way.

The thesis

No cell acts alone, and no target does either.

A target is not a molecule. It is a molecule in a cell, in a neighbourhood, in a tissue under a particular set of conditions — and change any of those and the intervention changes with it. The field has spent a decade getting very good at the first noun. We are working on the sentence.

Four commitments
I

Multimodal

Every section reads from a different part. Transcriptomic, proteomic, epigenomic, spatial — one performance.

II

Multi-scale

The phrase, the passage, the whole work. Molecular through tissue, held in a single model.

III

Causal

We study what happens when the conductor changes something. Perturbation, not observation.

IV

Mechanistic

A result you cannot trace to a mechanism is applause without a performance.

What we are building
I. Overture

The virtual tissue

Multimodal, multi-scale, causal and mechanistic models of living tissue — the substrate everything else is built on.

II. Andante

Target discovery

Interventions simulated in context, then ranked by tissue-level effect, tractability and translational risk.

III. Finale

Clinical translation

The models are judged on one question: do they change which molecules reach patients, and whether those molecules work.
