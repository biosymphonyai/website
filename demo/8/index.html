Orchestrating the signals of life

Health is a phase relationship.

Two waves: the tissue as measured, and the tissue as simulated. When they are in phase you understand the system well enough to intervene in it. Everything we build is an attempt to close that gap and keep it closed under perturbation.

The thesis

Disease is not a broken part. It is a system out of tune.

Which is why single-target thinking keeps disappointing. Loss of function in one node is usually absorbed by a tissue that has redundancy and feedback designed in. The interventions that work are the ones that shift the whole system's operating point — and finding those requires modelling the system, not enumerating its parts.

Four commitments
01

Multimodal

Each modality is one measurement of the same underlying dynamics. Fusing them is how the phase becomes recoverable.

02

Multi-scale

Coupling across scales is where the interesting behaviour lives — resonance between molecular and tissue timescales.

03

Causal

Perturbation tells you the transfer function. Observation only ever tells you the output.

04

Mechanistic

We want to know which coupling produced the effect, because that is the thing you can act on.

What we are building
Model

The virtual tissue

Multimodal, multi-scale, causal and mechanistic. A tissue you can perturb a thousand times before you perturb a real one.

Discovery

Targets by tissue response

Target discovery scored on simulated system-level effect across multi-omics, not on differential expression in isolation.

Loop

Simulate, measure, correct

Every wet-lab result is a phase error we feed back. The platform is the loop, not any single model.
